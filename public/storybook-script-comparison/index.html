Why the storybook stopped at preparation

With the same two reference images, the original script repeatedly failed and the revised script completed. The strongest script-level difference is that the original stages supporting characters as visible actors, while the revision explicitly maps the two reference images and limits who is depicted individually.

Leading hypothesis, not a confirmed root cause

The preparation step may have counted one or more supporting identities as part of the illustrated cast, or failed to map the two references unambiguously. The reported two-versus-three reference prompts are consistent with this possibility. The generic error does not identify the rejected field, the counted character, or the failing validation step. The successful rewrite changed several variables together.

2. What we changed

  1. Mapped each reference: image 1 = Padi; image 2 = Aunt Rahab. The header names both characters and describes their clothing.
  2. Defined the illustrated cast: only Padi and Aunt Rahab should be depicted individually. Others may appear as indistinct background figures. The revision explicitly says not to depict God as a visible character; there is no evidence that this caused the original error.
  3. Moved the spies out of staged action: Rahab retells the rooftop escape and the promise; in the rescue scene, the guides aren’t individually shown.
  4. Reduced competing subjects: shifted the cousin’s and family’s lines to Padi, kept the procession distant, and used the horns and shout as off-screen cues instead of foregrounding the priests and Joshua.
  5. Reorganized the narrative: added a title, clarified the viewpoint, and redistributed several events across pages. These are additional differences, not proven fixes.
Start with pages 3–5 and 19. The spies enter, hide, escape, speak, hand over the cord, and return to guide the family. This is the strongest recurring-character cue in the original. Page 9 and page 15 add distinct speakers, but neither is a confirmed trigger.

3. Compare each page

Open any page to read the exact text side by side. Same-numbered pages are aligned for inspection, but some events moved in the rewrite.

P01 Padi and the city walls

Both versions introduce Padi. The revision gives the illustrator one clear action: Padi looking up at the walls.

Original

Padi was ten years old, living in Jericho. His city had the thickest stone walls in the world. Everyone said nothing could knock them down, but lately, the city shook with fear.

Modified

Padi was ten years old and lived in Jericho. Every morning, he looked up at the enormous stone walls surrounding his city. They seemed so strong that nothing could ever knock them down.

P02 Aunt Rahab and the distant camp

The original introduces Rahab in exposition; the revision puts both leads together at the window and sets the camp in the distance.

Original

They feared the Israelites camped across the river. Padi heard stories of how their God parted the Red Sea. Jericho trusted its walls, but Aunt Rahab said stone was nothing compared to the true God.

Modified

Padi’s Aunt Rahab lived in a house built into the city wall. One evening, Padi stood beside her window. Beyond the walls, a distant camp stretched across the land. Something important was about to happen.

P03 The spies enter; the king sends guards Cast cue

First strong cue for extra visible actors: two spies enter and guards pursue them. The rewrite makes the danger a conversation between Padi and Rahab; it does not stage the king or guards.

Original

Days ago, Aunt Rahab did something incredibly brave. Two Israelite spies came into the city. The King of Jericho sent guards to hunt them down. If caught, it meant total trouble.

Modified

“Why is everyone afraid?” Padi asked. “People have heard what God has done for the Israelites,” Aunt Rahab replied. “He made a path through the sea. These walls are not stronger than Him.”

P04 The rooftop hiding scene Cast cue

The original gives the spies a specific physical setting and action with Rahab. The revision makes their escape something Rahab tells Padi about.

Original

Aunt Rahab trusted God before even meeting His people. She hid the spies on her roof under flax plants and told the guards they left. She risked everything because she had faith.

Modified

Aunt Rahab told Padi how she had helped two Israelite visitors escape danger. She believed in their God and had protected them when they needed help. Now, their promise gave her hope.

P05 The rope escape and the scarlet cord Cast cue

The spies escape, hand over the cord, and speak directly. The revised illustration can show Rahab tying the cord as Padi watches; their instructions are relayed by Rahab. The shift to “our family” in the original is a minor viewpoint inconsistency, not a proven trigger.

Original

Before escaping down a rope, the spies promised to save our family. They gave her a scarlet cord. "Tie this red rope in your window," they said. "Gather inside, and God will protect you."

Modified

“They told me to hang a scarlet cord in this window,” Aunt Rahab explained. “When the Israelites enter Jericho, this cord will identify our house.” Padi watched her carefully fasten the bright red cord.

P06 Family gathers inside

The family is explicitly left in the background in the revision. Padi and Rahab remain the individually depicted subjects.

Original

Now, Padi's family squeezed inside Aunt Rahab's house, built right into the giant city wall. Padi looked at the window. The bright red cord hung there. It was their only hope.

Modified

Aunt Rahab gathered her family into the house. Padi stayed close beside her while the others waited quietly in the background. He looked at the scarlet cord, fluttering against the enormous stone wall.

P07 The first march

The army is reframed as a distant procession viewed from Padi’s window. A crowd mention by itself does not establish a reference requirement.

Original

Suddenly, loud trumpets echoed outside. Padi ran to the window. The Israelite army was marching! They weren't screaming or throwing stones; they were just walking quietly around the city walls.

Modified

Suddenly, trumpets sounded outside. Padi hurried to the window. Far below, a long procession moved around Jericho. The distant figures marched in silence while the sound of horns drifted through the air.

P08 Priests, horns, and the Ark

The original foregrounds the priests and the Ark as visual subjects. The revision foregrounds Padi and Rahab and keeps the trumpet sound.

Original

Priests carried a golden box called the Ark of the Covenant, blowing rams' horns. Toot! Toot! The sound made Padi's chest thump, but the soldiers behind them were completely silent.

Modified

Padi leaned forward to look. “Why aren’t they attacking?” he whispered. Aunt Rahab stood beside him. “Sometimes obeying God means doing something we do not understand yet.” Padi listened to the steady trumpet calls.

P09 A cousin speaks; the guards return Cast cue

A distinct relative gets dialogue and the guards speak again. The rewrite transfers the reaction to Padi and Rahab.

Original

They walked around Jericho once and left. "That's it?" Padi's cousin whispered. The guards on the walls laughed. "They are just walking!" they shouted. But Aunt Rahab told everyone to have faith.

Modified

The procession circled Jericho once and returned to its camp. “That was all?” Padi asked. “For today,” Aunt Rahab replied. The city walls remained standing, and the scarlet cord swayed gently outside their window.

P10 The second march

The original already centers the two leads’ dialogue. The revised version makes the marching group distant and standardizes Aunt Rahab’s name.

Original

On Day Two, trumpets sounded again. The Israelites marched quietly around the city. "Why are they doing that?" Padi asked, holding Rahab's hand. "They are obeying their God," she whispered. "Trust Him."

Modified

On the second morning, the trumpets sounded again. Padi took Aunt Rahab’s hand as they watched the distant procession circle the city. “They are doing the same thing,” he said. “They are being faithful,” she answered.

P11 Days three and four

Both compress repeated activity. The rewrite centers Padi checking the cord and does not reintroduce the guards through their reaction.

Original

Day Three and Day Four passed the same way-one quiet trip daily. The silence was getting spooky, and the laughing stopped. Inside, Padi looked at the scarlet cord, remembering God saw them.

Modified

The third day passed, then the fourth. Each day brought one quiet march around Jericho. Padi began to recognize the rhythm of the horns. Each evening, he checked that the scarlet cord was still secure.

P12 Family members ask about safety

The revision gives the question to Padi instead of creating an additional speaking role for a family member.

Original

On Day Five and Day Six, the Israelites kept marching. Family members grew super nervous. "Are we safe in the wall?" they asked. Aunt Rahab nodded. "God is stronger than stone. Have faith."

Modified

By the fifth day, waiting felt difficult. “What if the walls fall?” Padi asked. Aunt Rahab knelt beside him. “We will stay together, just as we were told.” Padi held her hand a little tighter.

P13 The seventh day begins

The original moves directly to the army’s repeated circles. The revision uses this page for a sixth-day conversation; the seventh-day action shifts to pages 14–15.

Original

Sun rose on Day Seven. Something was totally different. The Israelites didn't stop after one circle. They kept walking! They completed a second circle, then a third, marching in absolute silence.

Modified

On the sixth evening, golden light filled the room. Padi sat beside Aunt Rahab near the window. “I’m still frightened,” he admitted. “Being brave does not mean never feeling afraid,” she said. “We can be afraid and still trust.”

P14 Priests prepare to blow the horns

The rewrite shows Padi and Rahab watching the distant procession. The original’s foreground horn action is replaced by a later sound cue.

Original

They circled Jericho six times! Padi's heart thwacked with excitement and fear. On the seventh time around, the entire army stopped. The world held its breath as priests lifted horns to their lips.

Modified

Before sunrise on the seventh day, the trumpets began again. Padi and Aunt Rahab watched the distant procession complete one circle. But this time, it did not stop. A second circle began, followed by a third.

P15 Joshua gives an order Cast cue

Joshua is explicitly named, identified, and given dialogue in the original. The rewrite keeps the countdown here and turns the shout into an off-screen sound on page 16.

Original

BLAAST! The horns blew louder than ever. Joshua, Israel's leader, shouted, "Shout! For the Lord has given you the city!" Thousands of soldiers opened their mouths and let out a mighty roar.

Modified

Padi counted carefully as the procession continued. “Four… five… six…” Then came a seventh circle. Padi and Aunt Rahab stood together beside the window. Outside, the whole city seemed to be holding its breath.

P16 The ground shakes

Both focus on Padi and Rahab. The revision builds up to the wall collapse on the next page.

Original

CRACK! A sound like thunder ripped through the air. The ground rolled and shook. Padi grabbed Aunt Rahab's arm as the giant, unbreakable walls began to crumble like dry crackers!

Modified

A tremendous trumpet blast filled the air. Then a mighty shout rose from beyond the walls. The floor trembled beneath Padi’s sandals. He stepped away from the window and reached for Aunt Rahab’s arm.

P17 The walls collapse

Both preserve the event. The revision anchors the viewpoint at the window with Padi and Rahab physically present.

Original

Huge stones crashed down in giant clouds of dust. Left and right, the mighty walls tumbled to the earth. Noise was deafening, but inside their room, nothing was moving at all.

Modified

CRACK! Beyond their window, huge stones began to tumble. Dust rolled across the city as the walls collapsed. Aunt Rahab held Padi close. He shut his eyes while the crashing echoed around them.

P18 The house and cord survive

The outcome remains the same; the revision shows it through Padi’s reaction without introducing another character.

Original

It was a total miracle! The entire wall fell flat, except for the small piece holding Aunt Rahab's house. Their home stood perfectly still. The bright scarlet cord still hung safely in the window.

Modified

At last, the noise faded. Padi opened his eyes. Their room was still standing! The section of wall supporting Aunt Rahab’s house remained in place. Outside the window, the scarlet cord shone through the settling dust.

P19 The same two spies return Cast cue

The spies guide the family in the original, making them recurring visual actors. The revision has Padi walk beside Rahab, the family follow in the background, and the guides remain unidentified.

Original

The two spies ran through the thick dust, leading Padi's family out safely to join Israel. Jericho's strong walls couldn't save the city, but their faith in God kept them completely safe.

Modified

When it was safe to leave, Padi walked out beside Aunt Rahab. Their family followed in the background as they were guided toward the Israelite camp. Padi looked back once at the little house that had sheltered them.

P20 The ending

Both end with Padi and Rahab. The revision expresses the resolution through dialogue and Padi’s realization rather than an explicit closing moral.

Original

Padi smiled up at Aunt Rahab as they walked into a new future. He knew now that no matter how big or scary a problem looks, God is always bigger!

Modified

Padi smiled up at Aunt Rahab as they walked toward a new beginning. “I thought the walls were the strongest thing in Jericho,” he said. Aunt Rahab squeezed his hand. Padi understood now: their hope had never been in stone.

5. Troubleshooting and engineering follow-up

  1. Reproduce at one fixed setting. Use the same Script to Storybook build, mode, two image files, upload order, and 8- or 20-page setting. Run the original and modified inputs as controls. Record exact input text, output page setting, build, timestamp, and job/reference ID in the internal tracker.
  2. Test the mapping alone. Add only the two image-to-name mappings to the original. Leave the story and cast directions unchanged. If it completes repeatedly, mapping becomes a stronger explanation.
  3. Test cast scoping next. Keep the mapping and add only the two-lead depiction instruction. If it still fails, reframe only pages 3–5 and 19 so the spies are relayed rather than staged. Test the cousin and Joshua separately if needed. These are proposed runs, not reported results.
  4. Compare the preparation results. Check the detected character inventory, recurring-role flags, reference-index assignments, requested versus supplied references, first validation error, and whether illustration jobs were scheduled. Compare original and successful runs before attributing the failure to any one character.
  5. Improve recovery guidance. If a cast/reference mismatch is confirmed, show which characters were detected and which references were assigned. Offer a way to distinguish referenced leads from incidental figures and explain what to change instead of showing only a generic error.
Practical workaround

Use the tested modified script with one image per lead in the matching order. If the author wants a supporting character visibly recognizable across scenes, confirm that intent and test an explicit mapping rather than assuming they must remove the role.
